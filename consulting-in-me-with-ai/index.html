            Chapter 13 · 
            Build your name: be found, trusted and remembered.
            Six tools to turn good work into a name clients can find and describe: your positioning, your profile, a case study, a content plan, your relationships and the events you attend.
            • Say in one sentence who you help and how
            • Show proof clients can check, with consent
            • Grow relationships, not just a following
            
    
            Why your name matters
            Clients hire people they can describe.
            Good work does not speak for itself if nobody can say what you do. These six tools take you from a clear sentence about your niche to a small, honest body of proof, and a circle of people who know when to call you.
            
    
            Say it clearly
            One sentence on who you help, with what problem, and how. Everything else in your profile follows from it.
            Show it honestly
            Proof a client could check: anonymised case studies, practice notes and talks, shared with consent.
            Grow it slowly
            A light routine for relationships and events beats a burst of posting. Give first, follow up within 48 hours.
            
    
            Six tools, in this order
            Pick a step. Each one has a template, a filled-in example and a concept guide.
            Start with positioning, since the other five reuse it. Use the arrow keys to move along the steps.
            
    
            
    
            
    
            Three ground rules
            Visible does not mean boastful.
            
    
            Never inflate
            No results you did not achieve and no roles you did not hold. Every claim needs a proof point someone could check.
            Get consent first
            Anonymise client work, then send the draft to the client and get written permission before you publish anything.
            Give before you ask
            Share something useful with each person in your circle. Let AI help you draft, but never let it invent your proof.

            Consulting in Monitoring and Evaluation with AI Cheat sheet · Edition 2
            

            The CONSULT method
            C
            Choose the work: Choose before you chase.Find the real decision behind the ToR · Score fit, readiness, budget and conflicts · Ask clarifying questions before you bid
            O
            Offer: Lead with the answer you expect, then show how you will test it.Restate the problem in one sentence · Offer a first hypothesis · Break questions into a tree
            N
            Negotiate the contract: Contract the expectations, not just the price.Deliverables with page limits and review rounds · Payments on milestones, not approval · A written change process
            S
            Set up the inception: Design for use from day one.Meet the people who will use the findings · An evaluation matrix for every question · Sketch the report before collecting data
            U
            Uncover the evidence: Collect only what answers a question, and check it every day.Pilot every tool · Daily quality checks · Triangulate across sources
            L
            Land the findings: Answer first, then the evidence, then the detail.Answer first, then evidence · A one-page summary on top · Validate with stakeholders
            T
            Transfer and track use: The assignment ends when the findings are used, not when the invoice is paid.A management response with owners and dates · Hand over data and tools · Ask for feedback and a reference
            

            Trust = (credibility + reliability + intimacy) ÷ self-orientation. Maister, Green and Galford
            

            Read a ToR: ask first
            • Who will use the findings, for which decision?
            • Which questions matter most, and can they be answered in the time?
            • Is it monitoring, review, evaluation or impact?
            • What data already exists: baseline, monitoring, reports?
            • Is the budget stated, and does it match the work?
            • What are the deliverables, page limits and review rounds?
            • How is payment scheduled?
            • Who owns the data and the report?
            

            Contract the expectations
            ScopeThe agreed questions and what is excluded.DeliverablesEach one with format, length and review rounds.PaymentsMilestones tied to submission, with an advance.Change processExtra work is agreed in writing with days and fees.IndependenceThe client checks facts; the evaluator decides findings.DataWho owns, stores and deletes data, and how it is protected.AI useWhat tools may be used, on which data, and how it is disclosed.
            

            Write answer first: 1:3:25
            1 pageKey messages: the answer to each question, and what to do.3 pagesAn executive summary by question, with the main evidence.25 pagesFindings by question, methods, limits and recommendations. Annexes hold the rest.
            

            Eight habits of top firms
            Answer first · Hypothesis-driven · No overlaps, no gaps · 80/20 · So what? · Pre-wire · Obligation to dissent · Results, not reports.

            CBid or no-bid gate, Problem statement worksheet, Stakeholder power and interest mapOIssue tree and day-one hypothesis, Three-option proposal, Colour team reviewsNStatement of work and change control, RAPID decision roles, Engagement charterSKick-off and workplan, RAID log, Ghost deck and reportU80/20 analysis plan, Weekly status note, Evidence strength ratingLStoryline and action titles, Pre-wiring and validation, Impact and ease prioritisationTManagement response tracker, After-action review, Client feedback score
            

            With AI: six promises
            1. Client data stays where agreed. Only approved tools, only de-identified data, as the contract says.
            2. Every source is checked. No reference, quote or number goes in unopened.
            3. AI use is disclosed. The method note says what AI did and who checked it.
            4. Humans own the findings. AI drafts and suggests; the evaluator decides and signs.
            5. AI terms are in the contract. Permitted uses, data rules and disclosure, agreed up front.
            6. Saved time goes to the client. More listening, sharper synthesis, or a lower price.
            

            Get paid
            An advance on signing · Payments on submission, not approval · Invoice the day a milestone is met · Check registration, GST and tax deduction rules with an adviser.
