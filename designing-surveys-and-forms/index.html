      Every consent script says

        Collect less, protect more

        • Need it? If no decision uses a personal detail, do not collect it.
        • IDs, not names. Keep the list that links IDs to people separate and locked.
        • Hide small groups. Never report a group so small someone could be recognised.
        • Plan for distress. For sensitive topics, train interviewers and carry referral details.
        • Know your law. Data protection rules, such as GDPR or India's DPDP Act, cover monitoring data too.

        Before you write a question, look for one that has been tested. Copy it from the source document, adapt only what you must, and note the change.

                    Designing Surveys and Forms with AI Cheat sheet · HubForge playbook
                    

                    

                    First, decide
                    

                    Monitoring formSame people, as part of their work, again and again. Minutes, not a quarter of the visit.Evaluation surveyA sample, often with a comparison group, at baseline and endline. Core questions locked.
                    

                    The SURVEY method
                    S
                    Scope: Start from decisions, not questions.Name each decision, who makes it and when · One indicator per decision · Draw the empty table each one fills
                    U
                    Unpack into questions: Write it the way the person answering would say it.One idea per question · The words of whoever answers · A clear time period
                    R
                    Responses: Everyone finds exactly one answer that fits.Exhaustive options, no overlaps · Counts instead of vague words · Every scale point labelled
                    V
                    Validate the flow: Each person sees only what applies, in a sensible order.Consent first, sensitive later · Skip logic for follow-ups · Constraints on every number and date
                    E
                    Ethics and every language: Safe to answer, and the same meaning in every language.Only the personal data you need · IDs instead of names in reports · Two translations, reviewed together
                    Y
                    Yield-test: Real people use it before everyone does.Watch real people use it · Fix, then test the fixes · Checks that run every day or week
                    

                    Every answer takes four steps
                    Understand → Remember → Judge → Respond. Find the step a question trips on, and fix that.
                    

                    

                    

                    Ten wording rules
                    1. One idea per question. Were the staff polite? / Were the staff quick?
                    2. Use the respondent's words. Has the problem you called about been solved?
                    3. Give a time period. In the last 3 months, how many times did you visit the clinic?
                    4. Stay neutral. How useful or not useful was the training for your work?
                    5. Ask about the person, not everyone. Does anyone in your household use the toilet at home?
                    6. Avoid negatives. Should school fees go up, stay the same or go down?
                    7. Ask what people can know. Has the road to your village been repaired in the last year?
                    8. Avoid absolutes. Yesterday, before eating, did you wash your hands?
                    9. Keep it short. Which of these services did you receive?
                    10. Prefer tested questions. Use the Washington Group Short Set
                    

                    Answer options
                    • Counts beat vague words
                    • Exhaustive and exclusive
                    • Label every scale point
                    • Item-specific over agree-disagree
                    • Plan for not knowing
                    • Forced choice over check-all
                    • Fit recall to memory
                    • Keep open questions few
                    

                    Order: consent, screen, easy facts, core topic, sensitive with privacy, background, thanks.
                    

                    

                    

                    Modes, rules of thumb
                    Face to faceUp to about 45 minutes; use show cards for long lists and scalesPhone callUnder about 20 minutes; read no more than four or five optionsOnline, self-completedAbout 10 to 12 minutes; one question per screen on phonesSMSAbout 5 to 10 short questions; one idea per message, answered with a single numberVoice (IVR)About 5 minutes; two or three options per question, pressed as 1, 2, 3PaperAs short as possible; print skip instructions next to the answers ("if no, go to q8")
                    

                    With AI: six promises
                    1. Every question earns its place. Each one feeds a decision and a planned table.
                    2. No respondent data goes in. No names, phone numbers or raw answers in an AI tool unless your organisation approved it.
                    3. Standard questions come from the source. Copied from the official document, never from AI memory.
                    4. Translations are reviewed. Fluent reviewers decide, and every choice is noted.
                    5. Logic is tested on a device. By people walking every path before launch.
                    6. AI help is disclosed. The methods note says what AI drafted and who checked it.
                    

                    Before launch
                    • Every question feeds a decision or an evaluation question
                    • Standard modules copied from the source
                    • Options exhaustive, with honest exits
                    • Logic tested on a phone by three people
                    • Consent script and privacy plan agreed
                    • Pilot done; timings known
                    • Version number and change log in place
                    

                    In the field: daily checks by interviewer; back-check one in ten; separate codes for don't know, refused and not asked; share results back.
